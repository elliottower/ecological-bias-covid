\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am submitting the manuscript ``Ecological bias distorts effect sizes
in federated COVID-19 analysis: evidence from 42 million patient
records'' for consideration as a Research Article in \textit{BMC
Medical Research Methodology}.

This paper quantifies how ecological bias distorts effect sizes when
multi-site studies pool site-level summaries instead of individual
patient data.  Using 42 million COVID-19 records from the United
States (CDC) and Mexico, we show that ecological regression produces
linear slopes incommensurable with the individual-level odds ratios
they purport to estimate---a different quantity, not a noisy estimate.
Applied to the 4CE consortium's neurological dataset (21 hospitals, 6
countries), meta-analytic methods diverge by 350-fold, and a
multivariate consistency test detects coordinated heterogeneity
invisible to standard approaches.

The paper contributes to the methodological literature in three ways:
(1) it provides large-scale empirical quantification of ecological
bias in federated clinical data, a setting where the fallacy is
routinely acknowledged but rarely measured; (2) it introduces
structural diagnostics (a multivariate consistency test with a formal
degeneracy proof) for detecting when site-level pooling is
unreliable; and (3) it includes ten pre-registered supplementary
analyses with explicit falsification criteria, two of which fired,
demonstrating transparent reporting of both confirmatory and
disconfirmatory results.

The datasets used are publicly available (CDC Case Surveillance,
Mexico Datos Abiertos), all code is available on GitHub, and the
analysis protocol was frozen before results were examined.  The
manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere and is not under
consideration at another journal.

I believe this work is well suited to BMC Medical Research
Methodology's focus on statistical methods for health research, and I
hope the editors and reviewers will find it a useful contribution.

\closing{Sincerely,}
